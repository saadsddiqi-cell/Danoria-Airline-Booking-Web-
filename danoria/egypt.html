Nature

Gift of the Nile

The Sahara's Great Sand Sea, the fertile Nile Delta, the White Desert's surreal rock formations, and the Red Sea coral reefs of Sharm El Sheikh.

Nile River Sunset

The world's longest river (6,650km) flows through Egypt for 1,000km. Sunset felucca (sailboat) cruises between Luxor and Aswan pass banana groves, desert cliffs, and ancient temples.

Red Sea Coral Reefs

The Red Sea's reefs are among the world's healthiest — 1,200 fish species and 250 coral species in a sea with exceptional visibility (30m+). Sharm El Sheikh and Dahab are world-class dive destinations.

White Desert, Farafra

A surreal lunar landscape of chalk rock formations sculpted by wind erosion into mushrooms, arches, and abstract sculptures. Wild desert foxes roam at dawn and dusk.

Siwa Oasis

A remote desert oasis 300km from the Libyan border, famous for salt lakes, ancient oracle temples (where Alexander the Great was declared a god), and healing sulphur springs.

Architecture

4,500 Years of Civilization

The Pyramids of Giza and the Great Sphinx, Karnak Temple at Luxor, the Valley of the Kings, and Abu Simbel's colossal statues.

Pyramids of Giza

The only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World, built 4,500 years ago. The Great Pyramid of Khufu stood as the world's tallest structure for 3,800 years. The Sphinx guards it all with timeless stoicism.

Karnak Temple, Luxor

The world's largest religious complex, built over 2,000 years by 30 pharaohs. The Hypostyle Hall contains 134 massive sandstone columns. The Avenue of Sphinxes once linked Karnak to Luxor Temple.
