Nature

Seasons in Bloom

Mount Fuji's perfect volcanic cone, Arashiyama's bamboo grove, Hokkaido's lavender fields, and Okinawa's tropical coral reefs.

Mount Fuji

Japan's highest peak at 3,776m and most iconic symbol. A UNESCO World Heritage Site. Over 300,000 climbers summit annually during July-August. From Kawaguchiko, the reflection in the lake at dawn is sublime.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

A towering bamboo forest of Moso bamboo in western Kyoto. The eerie creaking sound as wind moves through 10,000 stalks is Japan's "100 Soundscapes." Best experienced at dawn before crowds arrive.

Tokyo at Night

Tokyo is the world's largest city (37 million metro area) and never sleeps. Shibuya Crossing, the neon-lit Shinjuku Kabukicho entertainment district, and teamLab digital art shows are unmissable.

Fushimi Inari Shrine

Ten thousand vermillion torii gates wind up a forested mountain south of Kyoto. Dedicated to Inari, the Shinto god of rice and prosperity. The 4-hour full trail circuit is Japan's most dramatic walk.

Architecture

Ancient Meets Future

Senso-ji (628 AD), Himeji Castle (UNESCO), Kyoto's 1,600 temples, and Tokyo's ultra-modern Shibuya Crossing skyscrapers.

Fushimi Inari Shrine, Kyoto

Founded in 711 AD, this is Japan's most visited Shinto shrine. The endless tunnels of orange torii gates donated by businesses seeking good fortune make it one of the world's most photographed religious sites.

Tokyo Skytree

The world's second-tallest structure at 634m (Tokyo's old radio call-sign). The two observation decks at 350m and 450m offer views of Mount Fuji on clear days, 100km away.
