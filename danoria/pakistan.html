Nature

Land of Giants

Home to K2 (world's 2nd highest peak), the serene Hunza Valley, fairy meadows of Nanga Parbat, and the crystal Saif-ul-Muluk lake.

K2 Base Camp

The world's second-highest peak at 8,611m. K2 is considered the most dangerous mountain on Earth, with a near 1-in-4 fatality rate for attempts. The Concordia base camp trek is a lifetime adventure.

Hunza Valley

Nestled in the heart of the Karakoram, Hunza Valley is famed for its crystal-blue Attabad Lake, 700-year-old Baltit Fort, and centenarian populations said to live longest in the world.

Fairy Meadows

A breathtaking plateau at 3,300m offering the most iconic view of Nanga Parbat, the "Killer Mountain." Accessible only by jeep and a steep 3-hour hike through dense pine forest.

Saif-ul-Muluk Lake

A glacial alpine lake in the Kaghan Valley, located at 3,224m above sea level. The lake is surrounded by mountains including the Malika Parbat peak and holds mythical significance in local folklore.

Architecture

Civilizations in Stone

From the 5,000-year-old Mohenjo-daro ruins to the Mughal Badshahi Mosque and the modernist Faisal Mosque — Pakistan is an architectural time capsule.

Faisal Mosque, Islamabad

Designed by Turkish architect Vedat Dalokay and completed in 1986, the Faisal Mosque is the national mosque of Pakistan. Its tent-like structure set against the Margalla Hills is iconic worldwide.

Badshahi Mosque, Lahore

Built in 1673 by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb, Badshahi Mosque was the world's largest mosque for 313 years. Its red sandstone courtyard can accommodate 100,000 worshippers.
